Design system · v1.3.0

Vice Night

A quiet, neutral base with Miami Vice cyan and pink as accents. Cyan does the working jobs: links, steps, flow, the edge of a zone. Pink marks what is special. The two meet in a thin cyan-to-pink stripe that recalls the Heat Vice jersey trim.

Palette

Each accent has a text-safe shade and a bright shade. The bright pair is for fills, edges and lines, never small text on the light background; text on a bright fill is ink.

Proportion

This keeps it from tipping into costume. If a screen has more pink than teal, pull the pink back.

Depth

For nested boxes in diagrams and dashboards. Containers step a little darker with each level of nesting, in a faint cool slate; the cards that hold the content sit on top in white. Dark mode reverses it: deeper means lighter. Always step one way, never alternate.

--bgLevel 0: the page
--depth-1Level 1: outer container (a host, a zone)
--depth-2Level 2: groups inside it (layers, bands)
--depth-3Level 3: cards, the things themselves
--depth-insetChips and code inside a card

  • One step per nesting level. Three container levels at most; past that, split the diagram.
  • Cards are always --depth-3, at any depth, so content reads as one layer.
  • Labels on --depth-1 and --depth-2 use --muted, --ink or --teal (4.58:1 or better). Pink text stays on cards.
  • A zone gets a cyan top edge. The card that is special gets a pink border on --pink-tint.
  • No pink text on depth fills in light mode: it falls to 4.07:1 on --depth-2, under the 4.5:1 minimum.
  • Don't tint the depth scale with teal. Teal means links and flow.

Status

Three states for cells, chips and marks on dashboards and matrices, in Miami hues: aqua, sunset orange and coral. Each has a text-safe mark and a soft tint.

--okAllowed · #006F7A on #D6F6F8 / #3DE0E8 on #0C3338
--warnWarn · #934600 on #FFE6CC / #FFB45C on #3D270C
--blockBlocked · #B92438 on #FFE1E3 / #FF7A85 on #451A20
  • Always with a word or an icon; color never carries the state on its own.
  • Status is a text mark or a tinted cell. Category colors are bars, outlines and tags, so the two never look alike.
  • "Blocked" is coral, not pink. Pink is kept for the special parts of a page.

Sequential ramp

Five steps from low to high for heat cells and bars: cyan for steps 1 to 4, pink for the top step. The label color for each step is a token too, so text on a cell stays readable in light and dark.

  • The value is always printed. Color is the second cue, and pink on the top step means "the highest".

Kit parts

vice-night-kit.css holds the parts every diagram page needs, as vn- classes. Each works on an HTML element and inside an SVG.

  • Cyan badges and flow lines carry the main path; the special step is pink.
  • The kit also sets the print rules: one landscape sheet (vn-letter or vn-tabloid on body), light on paper.

Symbols

vice-night-symbols.js adds eight line drawings to a page, so a diagram can draw its subject from shared parts. A symbol is a blank: the page puts real text on top of it. Symbols follow light, dark and print.

Page treatments

A treatment is the look of a whole page: its frame, where the title sits, and the drawing style. Pages in a set take different ones, so they are easy to tell apart. Set it with data-sheet on body.

Rules

  • Cyan and teal for links, focus rings, step badges, flow lines, zone edges, active states.
  • Pink for the special parts of a page, used sparingly: the card that matters, the special step, a "new" tag, the wordmark dot.
  • The gradient appears only as the top stripe or on one title word. On paper that word prints solid teal.
  • Big surfaces stay neutral. Buttons stay ink.
  • No full gradient backgrounds, neon glows, or palm-tree imagery.
  • No pink body text, and no bright cyan or bright pink text on the light background. Text on a bright fill is ink.

Use

Load the files pinned to a release. The first line is enough for the tokens alone; a page that cannot reach a CDN keeps a copy of the three files next to it. Every token and its value: vice-night.css.
